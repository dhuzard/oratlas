# Adolescent stress and persistent behavioural change

Adolescence is a period of substantial reorganisation in cortical and limbic
circuits [@spear2000]. Stress experienced during that window has been reported
to leave behavioural traces that outlast the exposure itself, which raises the
question of whether the effect is developmental timing or cumulative dose.

This short example review states three claims explicitly, so that each of them
is addressable both as a MyST cross-reference target and as an ORAtlas
interoperability record.

## Scope

The review is limited to rodent work using repeated, unpredictable stressors
administered before postnatal day 45, with behavioural readouts collected at
least four weeks after the last exposure.

:::{oratlas:claim} adolescent-window
:type: empirical

Repeated unpredictable stress applied during adolescence produces behavioural
changes that persist for at least four weeks after the last exposure
[@mccormick2010].
:::

The claim above is the review's central empirical position. Note that the claim
body is ordinary MyST content: the citation resolves normally, and the text
renders as regular prose in HTML, LaTeX and Typst output.
