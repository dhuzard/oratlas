# Results

Across the studies in scope, two findings recur often enough to be worth
stating as explicit claims, and one negative result is worth stating in the
same way so that it can be challenged on the same terms.

## Mechanism

:::{oratlas:claim} hpa-axis-mediation
:type: mechanistic
:qualification: Rodent models only; evidence in humans is correlational.

Persistent behavioural change after adolescent stress is mediated in part by
lasting alterations in hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal axis reactivity
[@mccormick2010].

The mediation is partial: HPA reactivity accounts for some, but not all, of the
variance in later behaviour.
:::

The mechanistic claim above spans two paragraphs. Both are part of the claim,
and both appear in the exported claim text.

## Timing versus dose

Total stressor exposure is often confounded with developmental timing, because
adolescent protocols are typically longer than the adult protocols they are
compared against.

:::{oratlas:claim} timing-not-dose
:type: empirical

Studies that equate total stressor exposure between adolescent and adult
animals still report larger persistent effects in the adolescent group, so the
effect is not explained by cumulative dose alone.
:::

## What the evidence does not support

Sensitive-period accounts of early adversity are sometimes extended to
adolescence by analogy with earlier developmental windows [@andersen2008]. The
evidence in scope here does not support that extension, and the claim below
states that limit explicitly rather than leaving it implicit.

:::{oratlas:claim} no-single-critical-period
:type: summary
:qualification: Applies to the rodent literature in scope; see the scope section.

The reviewed evidence does not identify a single narrow critical period within
adolescence; effects are observed across the whole adolescent window rather
than at one discrete age.
:::
